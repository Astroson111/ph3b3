# Emergency Communications Quick Card

**If you have a phone and any signal: call 911. Text 911 works in many US counties if voice fails.**

## Calling for help by radio — listen first, then call

| Service | Channel / Frequency | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| Amateur 2m (VHF) | **146.520 MHz FM** | National simplex calling frequency. Most monitored ham freq in the US. License normally required; in a genuine life-safety emergency any person may use any means (47 CFR 97.403, 97.405). |
| Amateur 70cm (UHF) | **446.000 MHz FM** | National simplex calling frequency. |
| Marine VHF | **Channel 16 (156.800 MHz)** | International distress/calling. Coast Guard monitors. |
| CB | **Channel 9 (27.065 MHz)** | Emergency/traveler assistance. Ch 19 is highway chatter. |
| FRS/GMRS | **Channel 20 (462.675 MHz)** on GMRS, Ch 1 by convention | No single national standard; 462.675 is the traditional GMRS travel/emergency channel. |
| MURS | **Ch 3 (151.940 MHz)** | Low use; try if others silent. |
| Local repeaters | See repeater list in this library | Say the repeater name, then "emergency traffic." |

## How to call

1. Listen 30 seconds. Do not talk over anyone.
2. Say: **"MAYDAY, MAYDAY, MAYDAY"** (life-threatening) or **"PAN-PAN, PAN-PAN, PAN-PAN"** (urgent, not immediately life-threatening), then "any station, any station."
3. Give: **who** you are (callsign or name), **where** you are (road, landmark, GPS if you have it), **what** is wrong, **how many** people, **what you need**.
4. Release the key and **wait 10–15 seconds**. Repeat every minute.
5. If no answer after three tries, change to the next service in the table.

## Other signals

- **NOAA Weather Radio:** 162.400–162.550 MHz (7 channels). Receive-only; alerts and emergency instructions.
- **Visual:** three of anything (fires, flashes, whistle blasts) is the distress signal. SOS = · · · — — — · · ·
- **Ground-to-air:** V = need assistance, X = need medical, arrow = traveling this direction.
